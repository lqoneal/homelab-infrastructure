# Engineering Operating System (EOS) Constitution

---

# Preamble

Engineering knowledge is a long-lived organizational asset whose value depends upon its accuracy, continuity, traceability, and accessibility.

The Engineering Operating System (EOS) exists to preserve that knowledge independently of individual engineers, software implementations, computing environments, or organizational change.

EOS establishes the constitutional principles, governance structure, and engineering discipline necessary to ensure that engineering work remains reproducible, resumable, verifiable, and continuously improvable throughout its lifecycle.

All subordinate engineering artifacts derive their authority from this Constitution.

---

# Article I - Purpose

The purpose of EOS is to provide a durable engineering framework for governing engineering knowledge and engineering work.

EOS exists to ensure that engineering efforts:

- remain understandable;
- remain reproducible;
- remain resumable;
- remain traceable;
- remain governable;
- remain continuously improvable.

EOS governs engineering information rather than specific software implementations.

---

# Article II - Mission

The mission of EOS is:

> To make every engineering effort reproducible, resumable, traceable, validated, and continuously improvable through a single authoritative engineering system.

---

# Article III - Vision

The vision of EOS is:

> Any qualified engineer or intelligent system SHALL be capable of reconstructing the complete engineering context of any EOS-managed project using only authoritative EOS records.

Engineering continuity SHALL NOT depend upon human memory.

---

# Article IV - Core Values

EOS is governed by the following enduring values.

## Integrity

Engineering records shall accurately represent engineering reality.

---

## Clarity

Engineering information shall be understandable by future engineers.

---

## Continuity

Engineering knowledge shall survive personnel changes, hardware replacement, software evolution, and organizational change.

---

## Reproducibility

Engineering work shall be reproducible through documented processes.

---

## Stewardship

Engineering artifacts shall be preserved as organizational knowledge.

---

# Article V - Engineering Principles

## Principle 1
### One Authoritative Source

Every engineering artifact SHALL have exactly one authoritative source.

Duplicate authoritative records SHALL NOT exist.

---

## Principle 2
### Capture Once, Publish Many

Engineering information SHALL be captured once and reused wherever required.

Generated views SHALL derive from authoritative records.

---

## Principle 3
### Views Are Not Records

Reports, dashboards, summaries, AI responses, and generated outputs SHALL NOT become authoritative records.

---

## Principle 4
### Inventory Before Design

Existing engineering capabilities SHALL be inventoried before creating new capabilities.

---

## Principle 5
### Architecture Before Implementation

Major engineering capabilities SHALL be architected before implementation begins.

---

## Principle 6
### Validation Before Publication

Engineering work SHALL be validated before publication.

---

## Principle 7
### Resumability by Design

Every long-running engineering activity SHALL be resumable from authoritative engineering records.

---

## Principle 8
### Platform Before Project

Reusable platform capabilities SHOULD be preferred over project-specific implementations.

---

## Principle 9
### Single Implementation, Multiple Interfaces

Capabilities SHALL have a single authoritative implementation.

Multiple interfaces MAY expose the same capability.

---

## Principle 10
### Traceability

Engineering decisions, specifications, implementations, validations, and records SHALL be traceable through explicit relationships.

---

## Principle 11
### Relationships Are First-Class

Relationships between engineering artifacts SHALL be treated as authoritative engineering information.

---

## Principle 12
### Engineering Continuity Over Convenience

Engineering continuity SHALL take precedence over short-term implementation convenience.

---

## Principle 13
### Correctness Over Momentum

Long-term architectural correctness SHALL take precedence over implementation speed.

Previously completed work MAY be revised when doing so improves architectural integrity and maintains traceability.

---

# Article VI - Governance Hierarchy

EOS governs engineering through a hierarchical system of authority.

Authority SHALL flow downward through the following levels.

1. Constitution
2. Engineering Decisions
3. Specifications
4. Standards
5. Services
6. Validation
7. Implementations

Lower-level artifacts SHALL NOT conflict with higher-level artifacts.

Conflicts SHALL be resolved by revising the lower-level artifact or formally amending the governing artifact.

---

# Article VII - Authoritative Engineering Records

Engineering knowledge SHALL be preserved through authoritative records.

Every authoritative record SHALL possess:

- a unique identifier;
- an accountable owner;
- defined governance;
- revision history;
- traceability;
- validation status.

Authoritative records SHALL remain implementation-independent.

---

# Article VIII - Engineering Lifecycle

Engineering work SHALL follow a disciplined lifecycle.

1. Identify
2. Inventory
3. Govern
4. Specify
5. Implement
6. Validate
7. Publish
8. Preserve

Engineering work SHALL NOT be considered complete until the lifecycle has been satisfied.

---

# Article IX - Definition of Done

An engineering capability SHALL be considered complete only when:

- the problem has been defined;
- existing capability has been inventoried;
- governing decisions have been recorded;
- specifications have been approved;
- implementation has been completed;
- validation has been performed;
- documentation has been updated;
- engineering context reconstruction has been updated;
- preservation requirements have been satisfied.

---

# Article X - Engineering Rights

Every authoritative engineering artifact possesses the right to:

- one authoritative source;
- one unique identity;
- explicit governance;
- traceability;
- validation;
- historical preservation;
- reconstruction through EOS services.

These rights SHALL NOT be removed without constitutional amendment.

---

# Article XI - Constitutional Amendment

This Constitution is intended to remain stable.

Amendments SHALL occur only when necessary to improve the long-term governance of EOS.

Architectural evolution SHOULD normally occur through subordinate engineering decisions and specifications rather than constitutional amendment.

Every amendment SHALL:

- identify the reason for change;
- identify affected constitutional articles;
- preserve historical versions;
- remain traceable.

---

# Ratification

This Constitution establishes the governing principles of the Engineering Operating System (EOS).

All subordinate engineering artifacts derive their authority from this document.

By adopting this Constitution, the EOS Program commits to preserving engineering knowledge as a durable organizational asset through disciplined governance, authoritative records, reproducible processes, and continuous improvement.

This Constitution shall remain the highest governing artifact of the Engineering Operating System unless formally superseded through constitutional amendment.
